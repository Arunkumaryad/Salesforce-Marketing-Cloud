# 1A. Types of Lists (Overview with Step-by-Step Examples)

Salesforce Marketing Cloud Engagement uses two main list types to control **who receives an email** and **who does not**: **Publication Lists** and **Suppression Lists**.

## Publication Lists

1. Publication lists control which emails your subscribers receive. Publication lists allow subscribers to control what types of communications they receive, such as opting out of future sends from a particular list (it helps to imagine an email preference center here).
2. If no Publication List is selected at send time, the **All-Subscribers** list is used by default. You can find an All-Subscribers list as well as Publication lists in Marketing Cloud's Email Studio.

### Step-by-step example – Publication List

**Scenario:** A company sends three categories of email — *Newsletters*, *Promotions*, and *Invoices*. A subscriber wants Invoices but not Promotions.

| Step | Action | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Go to **Email Studio → Subscribers → Publication Lists**. | Publication Lists page opens. |
| 2 | Click **Create** and add a list named `Promotions`. | `Promotions` publication list is created. |
| 3 | Repeat for `Newsletters` and `Invoice Communications`. | Three publication lists now exist. |
| 4 | Enable **Public** on each list so subscribers can manage them in the Subscription Center. | Subscribers can self-select preferences. |
| 5 | The subscriber unchecks `Promotions` in the Subscription Center. | Subscriber is opted out of Promotions only. |
| 6 | At send time, choose `Invoice Communications` as the Publication List for the Invoice email job. | Only subscribers still opted in to `Invoice Communications` are eligible for that send. |
| 7 | If no Publication List is selected for a send, Marketing Cloud defaults to `All Subscribers`. | Every non-globally-unsubscribed subscriber is eligible. |

**Result:** The subscriber keeps receiving Invoice emails but no longer receives Promotions — without unsubscribing from everything.

## Suppression Lists

1. Suppression lists are subsets of subscribers that you choose not to send certain communications to. These lists filter out email addresses to prevent them from receiving your communications. They act as a **"do not contact"** list for your email campaigns.
2. Selecting suppressions for guided sends, user-initiated, and triggered emails in Email Studio is simple. Whichever method you opt for, choose the suppression list from the audience menu before you hit send.

### Step-by-step example – Suppression List

**Scenario:** Five records exist in the target Data Extension (`SK001`–`SK005`). `SK003` and `SK005` have previously complained about spam and must never receive email.

| Step | Action | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Go to **Email Studio → Subscribers → Suppression Lists**. | Suppression Lists page opens. |
| 2 | Click **Create** and name the list `Spam_Complaints_Suppression`. | An empty suppression list is created. |
| 3 | Import or add `SK003` and `SK005` to the list (by SubscriberKey or Email Address). | The suppression list now contains 2 records. |
| 4 | Open the email job in **Email Studio** and click **Send**. | The Send/Audience wizard opens. |
| 5 | Under **Select Audience**, choose the target Data Extension (5 records) as normal. | Target audience = 5 records. |
| 6 | In the audience menu, add `Spam_Complaints_Suppression` as the suppression list before sending. | Suppression list is attached to the send. |
| 7 | Complete **Configure Delivery** and send. | Marketing Cloud filters out `SK003` and `SK005` at send time. |

**Result:**

```text
Targeted        = 5
Suppressed      = 2  (SK003, SK005)
Actually Sent   = 3
```

## Quick Comparison of the Two List Types

| | Publication List | Suppression List |
|---|---|---|
| Answers the question | "Does this subscriber want this type of communication?" | "Who must not receive this email at all?" |
| Controlled by | Subscriber preference (often via Subscription Center) | Marketer/business rule |
| Default if none selected | `All Subscribers` list is used | No suppression is applied |
| Where to manage | Email Studio → Subscribers → Publication Lists | Email Studio → Subscribers → Suppression Lists |
| Applied at send time via | Audience → Publication List selector | Audience → Suppression List selector |

# 2. Publication List

## Definition

A **Publication List** is a Marketing Cloud list used to manage a subscriber's preference for a particular **category/type of communication**.

In simple terms:

> A Publication List answers: **"Does this subscriber want to receive this type of communication?"**

Publication Lists are especially useful when a company sends multiple categories of email, for example:

- Marketing Promotions
- Product Updates
- Newsletters
- Transactional Notifications
- Offers
- Event Communications

A subscriber can opt out of a particular publication/category without necessarily opting out of every communication from the organization.

Salesforce documentation describes publication lists as a way to let subscribers opt out of a category of sends without opting out of all messages from an organization.

### Important point

A Publication List does **not** replace the target audience.

For example:

```text
Target Audience
      |
      v
CustomerProfile_Arun
      |
      v
Publication List
      |
      v
All Subscribers
```

The Data Extension determines **who is being targeted**, while the Publication List helps determine whether those subscribers are eligible for that category of communication.

---

# 3. Suppression List

## Definition

A **Suppression List** is a list of subscribers who should **not receive a particular communication**.

In simple terms:

> A Suppression List answers: **"Who must not receive this email?"**

Typical examples include:

- Customers who have complained about spam
- Competitors
- Cancelled customers
- Customers who have opted out of a specific communication
- Legacy unsubscribe records
- Internal/test addresses that should not receive production communication

Salesforce describes a suppression list as a list of subscribers that you don't want to receive communications. It filters subscriber keys and prevents those subscribers from receiving the message.

---

# 4. Publication List vs Suppression List

| Feature | Publication List | Suppression List |
|---|---|---|
| Main purpose | Manage communication category preferences | Prevent specific subscribers from receiving a send |
| Simple question | "Does the subscriber want this category?" | "Should this subscriber be blocked?" |
| Used for | Subscriber opt-out/category management | Do-not-contact/exclusion |
| Effect | Controls eligibility for the publication/category | Removes matching subscribers from the send |
| Example | Marketing Newsletter | Competitors |
| Subscriber relationship | Preference/subscription | Suppression/exclusion |
| Typical location | Email Studio → Subscribers → Publication Lists | Email Studio → Subscribers → Suppression Lists |

### Easy interview answer

**Publication List:**  
"A Publication List manages subscriber preferences for a particular category of communication."

**Suppression List:**  
"A Suppression List contains subscribers who must not receive a particular communication."

# 5. Important: All Subscribers

The screenshots show:

```text
Publication List: All Subscribers
```

**All Subscribers** is the master subscriber list used by Marketing Cloud for subscriber status and global unsubscribe handling.

When a send is configured with:

```text
Publication List = All Subscribers
```

the send is associated with the All Subscribers publication context.

### Do not confuse

```text
CustomerProfile_Arun
```

and

```text
All Subscribers
```

They have different purposes.

### CustomerProfile_Arun

This is the **target audience/Data Extension**.

It contains the subscribers/customers that the send is attempting to reach.

### All Subscribers

This is the **Publication List selected for the send**.

It applies subscriber-level subscription/unsubscription rules.

# 9. How Suppression Works During a Send

Suppose the target Data Extension contains:

```text
SubscriberKey
-----------

SK001
SK002
SK003
SK004
SK005
```

And the suppression list contains:

```text
SK003
SK005
```

The send effectively becomes:

```text
Target Audience
      |
      +---- SK001  → SEND
      +---- SK002  → SEND
      +---- SK003  → SUPPRESSED
      +---- SK004  → SEND
      +---- SK005  → SUPPRESSED
```

Therefore:

```text
Targeted = 5
Suppressed = 2
Potentially sent = 3
```

The actual final send count can still be lower because of other rules such as inactive subscriber status, duplicates, invalid addresses, exclusions, or other send-time filtering.

# 21. De-duplicate Subscribers

The Select Audience screen contains:

```text
De-duplicate subscribers
```

This option controls whether duplicate subscriber identities are removed from the send audience.

### Why it matters

Suppose the Data Extension contains:

```text
SubscriberKey | Email
--------------|----------------
SK001         | a@example.com
SK001         | a@example.com
SK002         | b@example.com
```

If the send de-duplicates by subscriber identity, the duplicate `SK001` record does not result in another email to the same subscriber.

### Interview answer

> De-duplication prevents multiple records representing the same subscriber from causing duplicate email delivery during a send.

# 37. Publication List vs Exclusion List vs Suppression List

These terms are commonly confused in interviews.

| Concept | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Publication List | Subscriber preference for a communication category |
| Suppression List | Subscribers who should not receive the communication |
| Exclusion List | Audience/list/data extension explicitly excluded from a particular send |
| Auto-Suppression List | Automatically applied suppression based on account configuration |

### Easy way to remember

```text
Publication = Preference
Suppression = Do Not Contact
Exclusion = Remove From This Send
Auto-Suppression = Automatically Remove
```

# 38. Common Mistakes

## Mistake 1 – Assuming Targeted = Sent

Wrong:

```text
Targeted = 10
Therefore Sent = 10
```

Correct:

```text
Targeted = 10
Final Sent can be lower
```

because subscribers can be filtered by:

- Duplicates
- Suppression
- Exclusion
- Subscriber status
- List Detective
- Other send-time rules

---

## Mistake 2 – Confusing Publication List With Data Extension

Wrong:

> "The Publication List contains my complete target audience."

Correct:

> "The Data Extension is the target audience, while the Publication List controls subscription/category eligibility."

---

## Mistake 3 – Assuming Suppression List Deletes the Subscriber

A suppression list does not mean the subscriber record is deleted from Marketing Cloud.

It prevents the subscriber from receiving the applicable communication.

---

## Mistake 4 – Ignoring SubscriberKey

SubscriberKey is extremely important when troubleshooting:

- Duplicate sends
- Missing sends
- Suppression
- Subscriber status
- Data Extension relationships

# 39. Troubleshooting Checklist

If an email is not sent to the expected number of subscribers, check the following.

### 1. Check Target Population

Verify:

```text
CustomerProfile_Arun
```

and confirm the number of records.

### 2. Check SubscriberKey

Look for duplicate SubscriberKeys.

### 3. Check Email Address

Verify that the email address exists and is valid.

### 4. Check Subscriber Status

Check whether the subscriber is:

```text
Active
Unsubscribed
Held
Bounced
```

Inactive subscribers can be excluded from sends.

### 5. Check Publication List

Verify the selected publication list:

```text
All Subscribers
```

or the intended custom publication list.

### 6. Check Suppression List

Make sure the subscriber is not present in the applied suppression list.

### 7. Check Exclusion

Check the:

```text
Excluded and Suppressed
```

section during send configuration.

### 8. Check De-duplication

Check:

```text
De-duplicate subscribers
```

and understand whether duplicate SubscriberKeys exist.

### 9. Check Tracking

Go to:

```text
Email → Tracking → My Tracking
```

# 41. Interview Questions and Answers

## Q1. What is a Publication List?

**Answer:**  
A Publication List is used to manage subscriber preferences for a specific category of communication. It allows subscribers to opt out of a particular type of email without necessarily opting out of all communications.

---

## Q2. What is a Suppression List?

**Answer:**  
A Suppression List contains subscribers who should not receive a particular communication. Matching subscriber keys are excluded from the applicable send.

---

## Q3. What is the difference between a Publication List and Suppression List?

**Answer:**  
A Publication List manages category-level subscription preferences, while a Suppression List explicitly prevents selected subscribers from receiving the communication.

---

## Q4. What is All Subscribers?

**Answer:**  
All Subscribers is the master subscriber list in Marketing Cloud. It maintains subscriber-level status and is commonly used as the publication list for email sends.

---

## Q5. Can a Data Extension and Publication List be used together?

**Answer:**  
Yes. A send can target a Data Extension while using a Publication List to manage the subscriber's publication preference.

---

## Q6. Does a Publication List define the audience?

**Answer:**  
Not by itself. The Data Extension, List, Group, or other audience source defines who is targeted. The Publication List controls subscription/category eligibility.

---

## Q7. What is a Sender Profile?

**Answer:**  
A Sender Profile defines the sender identity, including From Name, From Email, Reply Name, and Reply Email.

---

## Q8. What is a Delivery Profile?

**Answer:**  
A Delivery Profile defines technical delivery settings such as the sending IP address and default email header/footer.

---

## Q9. What is a Send Classification?

**Answer:**  
A Send Classification determines the sending classification used for the email and associates the Sender Profile and Delivery Profile.

---

## Q10. Why can Targeted = 10 but Sent = 1?

**Answer:**  
Because the initial target population can be reduced by duplicate filtering, suppression, exclusions, inactive subscriber status, invalid addresses, or other send-time rules. In the provided example, 9 subscribers were not sent because the tracking report showed `Duplicate (9)`.

---

## Q11. What does "Duplicate (9)" mean in Tracking?

**Answer:**  
It means nine targeted records were treated as duplicate subscriber records for that send, so only one subscriber record resulted in an email being sent.

---

## Q12. What is de-duplication?

**Answer:**  
De-duplication prevents duplicate subscriber identities from resulting in multiple email deliveries during a send.

---

## Q13. Does Delivery Rate 100% mean all targeted subscribers received the email?

**Answer:**  
No. In the screenshot, 10 were targeted but only 1 was sent. That 1 email was delivered successfully, so the delivery rate was 100%.

---

## Q14. What is the difference between Delivered and Sent?

**Answer:**  
Sent represents emails accepted for delivery by the sending process. Delivered represents messages that were successfully delivered without a bounce.

---

## Q15. What should you check if subscribers are missing from a send?

**Answer:**  

Check:

1. Subscriber status
2. SubscriberKey
3. Duplicate records
4. Suppression lists
5. Exclusion lists
6. Publication List
7. Sendable Data Extension configuration
8. Email address validity
9. List Detective
10. Tracking Not Sent reasons

---

## Q16. What is SubscriberKey?

**Answer:**  
SubscriberKey is the unique subscriber identifier used by Marketing Cloud to identify a subscriber. It is especially important for subscriber relationships, personalization, deduplication, suppression, and tracking.

---

## Q17. Can a subscriber be present in a Data Extension but not receive an email?

**Answer:**  
Yes. Being present in the target Data Extension does not guarantee delivery. The subscriber can be filtered by status, suppression, exclusion, duplicate handling, invalid address filtering, or other send rules.

---

## Q18. What is an Exclusion List?

**Answer:**  
An exclusion list is an audience/list/data extension that is explicitly removed from a particular send. It is different from a Publication List because it is used to exclude people from a specific send rather than manage a publication preference.

---

## Q19. What is Auto-Suppression?

**Answer:**  
An Auto-Suppression List is automatically applied to applicable sends based on the account's configuration. Subscribers matching the list are excluded without needing to manually add the list at each send.

---

```text
→ My Tracking
→ Open the Job
```


# 44. Best Practices

- Use meaningful names for Publication and Suppression Lists.
- Document the business purpose of each list.
- Use a consistent SubscriberKey strategy.
- Keep target audience and preference management conceptually separate.
- Review suppression/exclusion rules before sending.
- Check duplicate SubscriberKeys in sendable Data Extensions.
- Always review Tracking after an important send.
- Do not interpret `100% Delivery Rate` as `100% of the target audience was sent`.
- Use Tracking's Not Sent reasons when troubleshooting.
- Restrict access to suppression lists because they can affect who receives communications.
- Test with a small audience before production sends.
- Avoid sending production communication to internal test records unless required.
